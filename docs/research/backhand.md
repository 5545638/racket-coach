# Tennis backhand: published reference values for a racket-mounted IMU

Compiled 2026-11-23. Every number below was read in the fetched source text (full text, abstract, or a publisher/repository page). Several key PubMed/PMC/VU pages were blocked by bot checks, so some papers (Reid & Elliott 2002; Busuttil 2022 full text; Ruiz-Malagon 2025 full text) could not be read beyond their abstracts. Nothing was estimated.

Mapping caveat for all tables: most studies use optical motion capture. Their "racket angle" is relative to the lab frame (horizontal/vertical), usually from racket markers, which maps onto our face tilt / path elevation only loosely. Impact is defined differently in each study. Where a definition could not be read, this is stated.

## 6. Backswing / racket drop

Not found as racket orientation or lowest tip height. Related items only:
- Backswing separation angle (shoulder-hip): one-handed less than 30 deg, two-handed less than 20 deg (Reid & Elliott 2002, as quoted by Elliott 2006 Br J Sports Med 40:392-396, https://web.as.uky.edu/biology/faculty/cooper/Physics%20in%20sports-movement%20and%20health/Biomechanics%20and%20tennis.pdf).
- Separation angle at contact: one-handed +9.26 ± 7.26 deg; two-handed -6.47 ± 4.31 deg (Stepien et al. 2011, n=10 coaches; sign convention ambiguous).
- Short vs long backswing: peak impact force 330.0 ± 140.7 vs 180.8 ± 49.1 N (per Genevois review, citing Wu et al.).
- Racket drop height and face orientation at the lowest point: not found.

## 7. Follow-through

Not found numerically. Elliott & Christmass 1993: racket moved about 40 deg downward after impact (slice, both heights). Ruiz-Malagon 2025: one-handed BH has higher arm/forearm angular velocity about z, linked by authors to forearm extension and follow-through (no numbers read).

## 8. Elite vs intermediate / recreational

Not found for racket kinematics in the backhand. Only: Wu et al. contact duration did not differ between advanced and beginner groups; Knudson & Blackwell 1997 (via Elliott 2006): professionals about 4 rad/s wrist extension angular velocity vs intermediate players with tennis elbow about 0.4 rad/s flexion in one-handed BH drive (hand/wrist, not racket).

## 9. Variability

SDs are given in the tables above. Notably between-subject SD of racket speed at impact: 1.9-3.3 m/s (Zhang & Fujii, n=5); horizontal peak 2.0-2.7 m/s (Busuttil, n=16). Within-player SD: not found.

## 10. IMU / racket-sensor studies

- Ruiz-Malagon et al. 2025 (body-worn gyroscopes, n=20, see section 4). No racket-mounted values read.
- Racket-mounted sensor magnitudes for backhands (gyro deg/s, accel g): not found. Candidate not read due to blocked pages: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9699098, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8433919 (Sensors/PMC, content unverified).

## Gaps

- Racket angular velocity (deg/s), total or about long axis: none found.
- Face tilt at impact with known sign convention for one- and two-handed topspin/flat: none (Zhang & Fujii sign undefined).
- Two-handed backhand face angle, swing path angle, racket speed by skill: only Busuttil (components, sub-elite adolescent).
- Topspin vs flat vs slice head-to-head in tennis (not soft tennis): none.
- Backswing time, forward-swing time, peak angular acceleration timing, preparation time, racket drop: none.
- Reid & Elliott 2002 (Sports Biomechanics, one- and two-handed backhands) and Busuttil 2022 full text could not be accessed; likely contain racket data.
- Genevois review is secondary; its swing-time values are internally inconsistent.

## Coaching sources (not measured)

- Matsue & Maeda 2026 guideline values (face about 10 deg up with swing about +10 deg for topspin; slices face 20-35 deg up with swing about -10 to -15 deg) are the authors' recommendations from soft-tennis data, not tennis measurements.
- Elliott 2006 review (Br J Sports Med) is a narrative review.
