# Tennis forehand: published reference values

Compiled 2026-11-23. Only numbers read in a source are given. "not found" means I did not find it in the sources I could open.
Several PMC/PubMed pages were blocked by a bot check. Their abstracts were not read (Choppin 2011, Seeley 2011, Takahashi 1996 and Elliott 1989 primary papers, Genevois, Pedro 2026 full numeric tables).

Abbreviations: HP = high performance; MoCap = optical motion capture; CC = cross-court; DL = down-the-line.

## 4. Racket angular velocity (total and about the long axis)

Not found as racket angular velocity values in any source I could open.
- Landlinger 2010 reports segment angular velocities only (e.g. shoulder internal rotation 794 ± 83 deg/s elite, 791 ± 218 deg/s HP; elbow extension 278 ± 96 and 313 ± 49 deg/s; trunk rotation 745 ± 82 and 728 ± 72 deg/s; pelvis 541 ± 41 and 505 ± 63 deg/s). Not racket values. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3761808
- Pedro 2026: wrist angular velocity (flexion/extension, radial/ulnar deviation) is higher in HP near impact (p<0.001); no numbers in the text I read.
- Rigozzi 2023: device gyroscope range was ±4000 deg/s, but no angular velocity values are given.
- Forearm pronation/supination contribution to racket-centre speed: -0.2 ± 1.4 m/s (CC), 0.1 ± 1.1 m/s (IO), i.e. about 0; hand flexion/extension 1.0 ± 0.9 / 1.5 ± 1.7 m/s (Pedro 2022). This is a speed contribution, not deg/s.

## Grip (eastern / semi-western / western)

- Elbow at impact about 130 deg (eastern) vs about 100 deg (western); impact height about 4 cm below hip (eastern) vs at least 6 cm above hip (semi-western/western) (Reid et al. 2013, citing Elliott 1989/1997).
- Semi-western is preferred over continental for topspin because it makes the required racquet orientation easier (Elliott teaching article; qualitative).
- Rigozzi 2023 grip split (eastern 21, semi-western 17, continental 2) but no grip-wise results.
- Esser 2024 (Cureus 16(11):e74019, https://assets.cureus.com/uploads/original_article/pdf/310972/20241119-783882-j40enx.pdf): force at contact (hospital staff, 100 mostly non-players) was higher for eastern (16.57 ± 8.24 N early) than continental (15.30 ± 7.45) or semi-western (15.89 ± 6.86). Face position described only qualitatively (more closed for eastern and semi-western). Not a kinematic reference.
- Numeric face angle or swing path by grip: not found.
